Host a tournament

We're here to help you do it. Write to us and we'll send you all the information you need to run a Fermi Poker tournament, including questions and tips on writing your own. We may also be able to cover the cost of things like poker chips and dry-erase markers.

Write us at contact@fermi.org.

Step by step

  1. 1. Write to us

    Tell us where and when you'd like to hold your tournament and, if you already know, how many players you expect. We'll email you the rule sheets and questions.

  2. 2. Organize a room

    Ideally, each table seats up to six players with enough space between them to write down their guesses in secret.

  3. 3. Spread the word

    Put up posters, announce it in class, and consider using a sign-up list.

  4. 4. Print the materials

    Print one rule sheet per table, then print and cut out the question, hint and answer cards. Consider putting each question in its own envelope, along with its two hints and the answer. It looks more professional, and the cards don't get mixed up or lost. The cards are about 9.4 × 6.7 cm (3.7 × 2.7 inches), so look for envelopes that fit.

  5. 5. Get poker chips and something to write on

    Plan about 20 chips per player: a 500-chip set, around $60, is enough for 25 players. For writing down the guesses, notepads and pens work, as do mini-whiteboards or erasable cards with dry-erase markers.

  6. 6. Get water and snacks

    Optional, but welcome during a 2 to 3 hour tournament.

  7. 7. Find prizes

    Also optional, but they add to the excitement. You could ask a local business to sponsor one, such as gift cards or some of its products.

  8. 8. Plan the ante

    Decide how big it starts and how often it goes up. We can give you recommendations, but it depends on how long you want the tournament to take. If the tournament looks on track to run too long, raise the ante faster.

  9. 9. Run the tournament

    Open with a 2–3 minute introduction to Enrico Fermi and his life, explain the rules, and play. As players drop out, close tables when you can and move their players to the others, until only the final table is left.

  10. 10. Tell us how it went

    Send us a few photos, how many played and who won.

Poker chips

  • Plan about 20 chips per player.
  • A 500-chip set (around $60) is enough for 25 players.
  • Ask around: some people already have chips at home.

Tip

Does your school or university have a poker club? Host the tournament together with them.

Question cards

  • Print the questions from our print-and-play PDFs, which we email you after you contact us.
  • Use 150 gsm paper. Thicker works too, but isn't necessary.
  • 100 sheets (around $15) print 200 questions.
  • A table plays about 10 questions per hour.

Local questions

Write your own Fermi questions about your country, city or university.

Writing down guesses

  • Dry-erase pens with mini-whiteboards or dry-erase flash cards.
  • Couldn't get those in time? Pen and paper work too.

Venue

  • Classrooms work. Larger conference rooms are better.
  • Round tables are better than square ones for seating.
  • Space players out a bit, so everyone can write down their guess privately.

Tables

  • Ideally 6 players per table at most. 5 and 4 work well too.
  • Put at least one rule sheet on every table.

Freezeout or shootout?

Decide on a format before the event.

Recommended

Freezeout

As players drop out, move the others to fill empty seats and close tables. The constant rebalancing makes it faster and smoother.

Shootout

Every table plays until one or two players are left. They move on to a final table, or to semifinals first. Players who are out can stay seated and keep guessing, just without betting.

Before play starts

Plan 15 to 30 minutes for check-in and explaining the rules. Have drinks, and maybe some food, ready.
