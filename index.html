Celebrating Enrico Fermi's 125th birthday

Bring Fermi Poker to your school or university

This year marks the 125th birthday of Enrico Fermi. To honor him, we're planning to sponsor hundreds of Fermi Poker tournaments at schools and universities.

Fermi Poker is an estimation game that combines the emotional stakes and social dynamics of poker with the analytical yet creative skill of Fermi estimation. Instead of being dealt random cards, you make guesses about questions that seem unknowable at first, like “How many dentists work in the US?” You write down a secret guess, bet on it, get helpful hints, bet again, and win by either having the best guess or by bluffing everyone else out.

With these tournaments, we at the Fermi Foundation want to make quantitative reasoning, world-modeling, Bayesian updating, Fermi estimation and calibration fun and social.

Interested in hosting your own Fermi Poker tournament? Write us at contact@fermi.org.

Educational value

Fermi Poker trains a cluster of skills that are genuinely useful outside the game.

Fermi estimation

Breaking a seemingly unanswerable question into smaller parts you can actually reason about. Physicists use it to check whether an experiment is even feasible before spending months on it, entrepreneurs to size a market, and anyone can use it to tell whether a number in the news makes sense.

Calibration

Developing an accurate sense of what you know and what you don’t. A range that’s too narrow risks missing entirely; one that’s too wide rarely wins. Over many rounds, players get better at putting numbers on their own uncertainty.

Bayesian updating

Changing your mind when the facts change is at the heart of rational thinking, and in Fermi Poker it comes up with every hint. Players learn to treat their beliefs as “hypotheses to be tested, not treasures to be guarded,” as Philip Tetlock puts it, and to neither overreact nor underreact to new evidence.

World-modeling

Every question is really about how the world is structured: how common different jobs are, how people spend their time, what things cost. The game rewards an accurate model of the world and helps you build one: every answer that surprises you shows where your model can be refined.

Cognitive empathy

Understanding what’s going on in other people’s heads. To play well, you have to model what each player knows, how much risk they’re willing to take, and how much each hint has shifted their confidence. Like a flight simulator, the game lets you practice this dozens of times in a few hours.

Knowing whom to trust

Figuring out whom to trust is most of the way to having correct beliefs. At the table, confidence and charisma say little about who estimates well. What counts is a track record of good estimates on similar questions, since expertise depends on the domain: the player who nails car questions may be lost on history questions. That’s worth remembering when influencers and celebrities weigh in on everything.

An antidote to number numbness

Douglas Hofstadter called it “number numbness”: the inability to grasp the size of large numbers, which he argued may be just as dangerous as illiteracy. Every question in Fermi Poker makes you weigh how big a number really is, until a million and a billion stop sounding alike.

Taking emotion seriously

Michael Nielsen has argued that we learn best when the emotional stakes are as high as possible without losing the ability to think calmly, like a game show contestant who loses the prize on a wrong answer and remembers the right one for life. Fermi Poker creates such moments all the time. Take the question “How many chickens are slaughtered for meat each year?” You’re sure it’s no more than 10 billion, so you go all-in, and then the answer comes in: over 75 billion. You’ll remember that far longer than if you’d heard it in a lecture.

Most of all, Fermi Poker is social. You meet new people, get to know others better, and find out how they think about all kinds of topics. Every question, hint and answer is a prompt for conversation, during the game and after it.

Frequently asked questions

Is it gambling?

No. By the usual definitions, gambling means staking something of real value on an outcome decided largely by chance. In Fermi Poker, you bet with poker chips that have no cash value, and the random playing cards are replaced by estimation questions, so it comes down to skill, not luck. That’s why the Fermi Poker app is rated 3+ on Google Play and 13+ on the App Store, while most poker apps are rated 18+.

What age is it for?

From about age 13 or 14, all the way through university.

Do you need to be good at math?

No. Fermi estimation has surprisingly little to do with what’s taught in math class. What counts is knowing a bit about how the world works and breaking a question into parts you can reason about; the arithmetic stays rough. So being bad at math doesn’t mean you’ll be bad at this.

Do you need to know poker?

No. The rules fit on one page, and the betting works like regular poker: check, bet or raise, call, fold, or go all-in. Read the rules.

Does it have to be in person?

Yes. The tournaments we sponsor take place in person.

How many players do you need?

In theory, six players at a single table are enough. For sponsorship, though, we mostly consider tournaments with 18 or more players. Smaller tournaments can still get everything else they need from us, like the rule sheets and questions.

How long does a tournament take?

Plan on 2 to 3 hours of play, plus time to set up. It depends on how many play and on the ante, the bet everyone pays for each question. If a tournament runs long, raise the ante faster to speed it up.

What does a host do?

Find a room and invite players. Print our rule sheets, one per table, and the question, hint and answer cards. Get poker chips, dry-erase markers and small whiteboards or cards for writing guesses, and maybe some food and drinks. On the day, open with a 2–3 minute introduction to Enrico Fermi and his life, then explain the game.

Is there support for hosts?

Yes. Once you get in touch, we email you the rule sheets and questions. If we sponsor your tournament, we also cover the cost of supplies, like poker chips, markers and whiteboards; the amount depends on the number of players. Write us at contact@fermi.org.

Can you charge an entry fee?

No. The tournaments we support must be free to enter. The only exception we might consider is a charity tournament where all proceeds go to a charitable cause.

Where do the questions come from?

Once you contact us, we email you PDFs with about 100 questions, each with its hints and answer. You can also write your own, for example about your country or city, and mix them in or use only yours.

When should it take place?

Any time from November, when our materials are ready. Ideally this year or early next year, while Fermi’s 125th birthday is still recent.

Why is it named after Enrico Fermi?

Fermi was famous for quick, accurate estimates from very little information, and he posed questions like these to his students, which is why they’re called Fermi questions. His best-known example asked how many piano tuners work in Chicago. Fermi Poker honors him on his 125th birthday.
